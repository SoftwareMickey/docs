Fake emails that imitate a service are common. A real Beaver email will never need you to hand over a secret, so you can check any message against four rules.

## What Beaver never asks for by email

Beaver will **never** ask you to send, reply with or type into a form linked from an email:

- your password,
- a 2-step verification code or a recovery code,
- an API key or access token,
- a card number.

A verification code email is the one place a code appears, and it is a code *you* just asked for — you type it into the Beaver screen you already have open, never into a page you reached from the email.

If a message asks for any of these, do not reply and do not click. [Report it](#report-a-suspicious-email).

## Check the sender

Real Beaver email arrives from the display name **Beaver**, from an address that begins `notify@notify.` and ends in Beaver's own domain. Two checks help:

- The part after the `@` must be Beaver's own domain, spelled exactly. Look-alikes (an extra word, a swapped letter, a different ending) are not Beaver.
- A display name alone proves nothing. Open the full sender address in your mail client.

## Check a link before you click

Every link in a Beaver email goes to one of two places: the Beaver dashboard you already sign in to, or the Beaver documentation site. To check one:

1. Hover over the button or link (or press and hold on a phone) and read the address your mail app shows.
2. Confirm the host is the same one you use to sign in to Beaver.
3. If in doubt, do not use the link. Open the dashboard yourself and look at **Notifications**: anything that needs your attention is there too.

Each email also prints the full address under its main button, so you can compare it without hovering.

## Why you received it

The footer of every Beaver email states why you got it — for example "You received this email because deployment notifications are enabled for Acme" — and whether you can turn that kind off. An email that gives no reason, or a reason that does not match anything you did or set up, is a warning sign. The full list of what Beaver sends is in [Emails Beaver sends](/operate/emails-beaver-sends).

## Report a suspicious email

Forward the message, with its full headers if your mail client allows, to **support@vectorihub.com**. If you already clicked a link or entered a password:

1. Change your Beaver password and, if you use one, your sign-in provider's password.
2. Revoke any API key you do not recognise and check your workspace's [audit history](/teams/audit-history) for activity you did not do.
3. Make sure 2-step verification is on.
