Every app you deploy on Beaver gets a **default address** the moment it is deployed. You can also
attach your own domain. This page covers both, and the one-time move of the default address from
`vectorihub.run` to `vectorihub.com`.

<Info>
  For the idea of how domains fit into a deployment, see [Domains and routing](/concepts/domains-and-routing).
  For exact command syntax, see [`beaver domain`](/cli/domain).
</Info>

## Your default address

A deployed app is reachable at `https://<name>.vectorihub.com`, with a TLS certificate issued and
renewed for you. Nothing to configure.

To see it or open it:

```bash
beaver containers      # lists your apps with their addresses
beaver open <name>     # opens the app in your browser
```

The address is also shown in your dashboard and in Beaver Desktop.

### How the name is chosen

- The name comes from your app's name, lowercased, with each run of characters that are not letters
  or digits replaced by a single hyphen, and any leading or trailing hyphen dropped.
- If that name is already taken, or is one Beaver keeps for itself (for example `api`, `www`,
  `docs`, `status`), Beaver adds a short random suffix, so you get something like `myapp-3fa91c2e`.
- An app keeps its address across redeploys. Redeploying does not change the name your users visit.

### Older deployment addresses

Each deployment of an app also has its own address, used to tell deployments apart in history.
Once a newer deployment replaces it, that older address stops answering. Always link to your app's
default address, never to an address copied from an older deployment. Beaver Desktop marks those
entries **no longer routed** so they are not mistaken for a working link.

## Use your own domain

Attach a domain you own to an app:

```bash
beaver domain add --container <name> --domain app.example.com
```

`beaver domain add` prints the DNS record to create at your DNS provider. Which record depends on the
kind of name:

| You attach | Record you create |
| --- | --- |
| A subdomain such as `app.example.com` | A **CNAME** pointing at the target Beaver gives you |
| A bare domain such as `example.com` | **A** record(s) pointing at the address(es) Beaver gives you |

Then check it:

```bash
beaver domain verify <domain-id>
beaver domain list --container <name>
```

Once DNS resolves, verification passes and the certificate is issued automatically. DNS changes can
take up to a few hours to be visible. If you use Cloudflare, set the records to **DNS only** (grey
cloud) so verification and certificate issuance can see them.

Your own domain is independent of the default address: your app stays reachable at its default
address as well, and removing a custom domain never affects your DNS records or your app.

## The move from `vectorihub.run` to `vectorihub.com`

`vectorihub.run` has expired and is not being renewed, so addresses ending in `.run` no longer
resolve. Beaver's default address is now under `vectorihub.com`.

**What you need to know**

- Your app keeps its name where it can. If that name is not available under the new domain, it gets
  a short suffix as described above. You will be told your new address before your app is moved.
- Anything that has your old `.run` address written into it needs updating. See the checklist below.
- Your own custom domains keep working. If your custom domain is a **subdomain** that you pointed
  with a CNAME at an old Beaver target, you need to make one DNS change, described next.

### If `beaver domain list` says "repoint DNS"

A subdomain custom domain used to be pointed at a Beaver target under the old `.run` name. That
target no longer resolves, so the domain shows **repoint DNS** in the `ACTION` column.

```bash
beaver domain repoint <domain-id>
```

This prints the one new record to create. Replace your old CNAME with it. Your domain, its
certificate and its history are kept; only the record changes. Verification runs automatically as
soon as the new record resolves.

This is a one-time change: the new target is a Beaver-owned name that does not depend on where your
app's default address lives, so it will not need to change again if the default address moves.

Bare domains (A records) and domains attached to a cloud are not affected.

### Checklist: what to check in your app

- **Hardcoded absolute URLs** to your app's old `.run` address, in code, config, emails or docs.
- **OAuth and SSO redirect URIs** registered with providers such as GitHub or Google.
- **CORS allowlists** and allowed-host settings inside your own app.
- **Webhooks** that other services send to your old `.run` address.
- **Code that reads the `Host` header** and compares it with a fixed name. During the move your app
  can receive requests carrying either the old or the new name; make sure it accepts both, or takes
  the name from configuration.
- **Bookmarks and monitors** pointing at the old address.
