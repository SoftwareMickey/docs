Signing in with Google proves who you are and nothing else: Beaver asks Google only for your
name, email address and profile picture (`openid email profile`), keeps no Google token, and
gets no access to your Google account or its data. Disconnecting Google later doesn't affect
your GitHub repository access, and the reverse.

Messages that are the same for every provider — *This email already has a Beaver account*,
*Sign-in cancelled*, *That sign-in link expired* — are explained in
[Troubleshooting GitHub](/guides/troubleshooting-github). The ones below are specific to Google.

## Signing in with Google

### Your Google email isn't verified

Google hasn't confirmed that you own the email address on this Google account, so Beaver can't
create an account from it. This is a check on Google's side, and it protects you: an account tied
to an address you haven't proven would be easy to take over.

- Verify the address with Google (Google sends it a confirmation), then try again; or
- sign up with email instead.

### Google didn't share an email address

Beaver needs an email address to create your account, and this Google account didn't provide one.
Sign up with email instead, or sign in with a Google account that has an email address.

### That's a different Google account

You were asked to confirm it's you with the Google account connected to your Beaver account — for
example, before adding a password — but you picked another one at Google's account chooser.
Start again and choose the Google account that's connected under **Settings → Security → Sign-in
methods**.

## Signing in from the command line or the desktop app

`beaver login` and Beaver Desktop don't show Google's page themselves — they open your **system
browser** at a Beaver approval page, where you choose Google, GitHub or email and approve the
device. That is deliberate: Google refuses to show its sign-in inside an embedded window (you'd
see *Error 403: disallowed_useragent*), so an app can't safely host it. If the browser can't
open (SSH, a server), run `beaver login --no-browser` and open the printed address on any device
signed in to the same account. See [`beaver login`](/cli/login).

## Good to know

- **Adding a password.** If Google is your only way to sign in, add a password from **Settings →
  Security** so a lost Google account never locks you out. You'll be asked to sign in with Google
  again first, because there's no current password to confirm.
- **Changing your Google account.** Use **Change Google account** in the same place. The new one is
  connected before the old one is removed, so you're never left without a way to sign in.
- **Your Google email changed.** Sign-in keeps working: Beaver identifies your Google account by its
  permanent ID, not by the address. Your Beaver email isn't changed because Google's was.
- **Removing Beaver from your Google account.** Beaver stores no Google token, so there's nothing
  to revoke. To also remove the entry from your Google account's third-party access list, use
  [Google's permissions page](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions).
- **The command line and desktop app.** Neither signs in with Google itself. `beaver login` and
  Beaver Desktop open your browser, where you sign in with Google, GitHub or email and approve the
  device.

## For operators and self-hosting

If you run your own Beaver, signing in with Google needs an OAuth client of your own. Most
"Google sign-in doesn't work" reports are one of these pages from Google, not a Beaver message:

| Google shows | It means | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Error 400: redirect_uri_mismatch* | The address Beaver sent isn't in the client's **Authorised redirect URIs** — usually a different scheme, host or a trailing slash | Register exactly `{your API address}/auth/google/callback`. The Beaver server prints the value to copy: run its `googlepreflight` command, or open **Users → Google configuration check** in the admin dashboard |
| *Access blocked* / *has not completed the Google verification process* / *can only be accessed by developer-approved testers* | The consent screen is still in **Testing** (limited to listed users) or awaiting review | Set the publishing status to **In production**; Beaver asks only for `openid`, `email` and `profile`, which need no extra verification |
| *Error 403: disallowed_useragent* | Sign-in was opened inside an embedded browser window | Use the system browser — the command line and desktop app already do |
| *Error 401: invalid_client* | The client id doesn't exist or belongs to a different project | Check `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_ID` |
| Beaver shows **Sign-in could not be completed** right after Google | The client **secret** is wrong or was rotated out, or the code was already used | Check `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_SECRET`; see the server log line `google exchange failed: class=…` |
| Beaver shows **Google is unavailable** | Beaver couldn't reach Google (discovery, keys or the token endpoint) | Check the server's outbound network access to `accounts.google.com` and `www.googleapis.com` |

Use a **separate client for each environment** (development, staging, production) so they never
share credentials, and keep the Beaver server's `OAUTH_STATE_SECRET` set to a long random value:
without it, sign-in is switched off in production. The settings to confirm in the Google Cloud
Console are: client type **Web application**; the exact redirect URI above and no JavaScript
origins; scopes `openid`, `email`, `profile` only; consent screen **External** and **In
production**; your privacy-policy and terms links live on an authorised, verified domain; and a
second client secret added (never swapped in place) when you rotate.
