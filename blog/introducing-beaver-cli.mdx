For the past several weeks, VectorIHub has been evolving from a deployment platform into a deployment orchestrator. Today, we're excited to introduce the next major step in that journey: **Beaver CLI**.

Beaver CLI is the execution engine that runs inside your infrastructure. It brings building, deploying, and operating applications directly to your servers, while VectorIHub coordinates everything from its control plane.

Unlike traditional deployment platforms, VectorIHub doesn't build your applications on shared infrastructure. Instead, your own infrastructure performs the build, ensuring your source code, compute resources, and architecture remain under your control.

## Why Beaver CLI?

Every deployment platform eventually faces the same question: where should builds happen?

Most platforms answer with: *"We'll build your application in our cloud."*

VectorIHub takes a different approach. With Beaver CLI, **your infrastructure builds your application.**

When a deployment starts, Beaver CLI runs on your server, clones your repository, builds the Docker image locally, deploys the container, and reports the deployment status back to VectorIHub.

This means:

- Your source code never leaves your infrastructure during builds.
- Your servers perform the build using your own compute resources.
- Images are built for your architecture automatically.
- VectorIHub orchestrates deployments without owning your infrastructure.

Your infrastructure stays yours.

## How it works

A deployment follows a simple flow:

```
Developer
   │
   │ Deploy
   ▼
VectorIHub Control Plane
   │
   │ Deployment Request
   ▼
──────────────────────────────
Your Infrastructure
──────────────────────────────
Beaver CLI
   ├── Clone repository
   ├── Build Docker image
   ├── Deploy container
   ├── Configure networking
   ├── Perform health checks
   └── Report deployment status
```

The control plane coordinates deployments. Beaver CLI executes them.

## Current commands

The first version of Beaver CLI focuses on authentication and project management.

| Command | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `login` | Authenticate your VectorIHub account and create a local session. |
| `logout` | Sign out and remove your local credentials. |
| `github` | Display the GitHub accounts connected to your VectorIHub workspace. |
| `help` | List available commands. |
| `exit` | Close the Beaver CLI session. |

## What's next: `connect`

The next major feature is one of the most important pieces of Beaver CLI's design.

Running `connect` on a VPS registers that machine with your VectorIHub workspace. Once connected, VectorIHub recognizes the server as deployment infrastructure and can orchestrate deployments to it.

The flow is simple:

```
VPS
 │
 beaver connect
 │
 Authenticate
 │
 Register Server
 │
 Generate Machine Identity
 │
 Establish Secure Connection
 │
 Ready for Deployments
```

After connecting, that server becomes available inside VectorIHub as one of your deployment targets. No agents hosted by VectorIHub, no managed compute — just your own infrastructure, connected to your workspace.

## A different philosophy

VectorIHub isn't trying to replace your infrastructure. It's designed to orchestrate it.

You decide where your applications run. You own the servers. You own the build process. You own the architecture. VectorIHub provides the orchestration layer that makes operating all of it simple through a single, consistent experience.

## Getting started

Launch Beaver CLI, then authenticate:

```bash
beaver
login
```

Connecting your server (`connect`) is coming next. From there, your infrastructure will be ready to build and deploy applications under VectorIHub's orchestration.

This is the beginning of Beaver CLI — and the foundation for a deployment platform built around a simple principle:

> **Your infrastructure. Your builds. Your deployments. VectorIHub simply orchestrates them.**
