A **workspace** is the container everything else in Beaver lives in: projects, deployments,
machines, managed services, secrets, domains, the activity log, and the plan you pay for.
Nothing in Beaver exists outside a workspace.

Everyone starts with a **personal workspace**, created when you sign up. A **team workspace**
is the same thing with more than one person in it. Each person has a [role](/concepts/roles-and-permissions)
that decides what they can do.

## Personal and team workspaces

| | Personal | Team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People | Just you | You and the people you invite, up to the seats on your plan |
| Plan | Free or Developer | Team |
| Roles | You are the Owner | Owner, Admin, Developer, Viewer |
| Activity log | Your own actions | Everyone's actions, attributed to who did them |

Moving from personal to team **converts the workspace in place**. When you buy the Team plan, the
workspace you already have becomes a team workspace: every project, machine, service, secret and
setting stays exactly where it is, and nothing is copied or migrated.

You can belong to several workspaces at once, for example your own personal workspace and your
company's team workspace. Each has its own plan, its own members and its own bill.

## What "the workspace owns" means

The workspace owns everything in it. The person who created a project, deployed an app or
added a machine does not own it, and it does not leave with them if they are removed or leave.
Beaver still records **who did what**, so an audit trail shows the person, their role at the
time, and what they touched. See [Access, audit and removal](/concepts/team-access-and-audit).

This is why billing belongs to the workspace, and why one person, the **Owner**, is
responsible for it. Ownership can be handed to another member; see
[Removing members and leaving](/guides/removing-members-and-leaving).

## The active workspace

If you belong to more than one workspace, each command, and each page in the dashboard or
Beaver Desktop, acts in exactly one of them: the **active workspace**. You choose it once and
Beaver keeps it, and it shows which workspace you are in whenever it is not your personal one.

Choosing a workspace never gives you access to it. Membership is checked on **every request**, so
if you are removed from a workspace, your next action in it is refused, even if you had it
selected. To someone who is not a member, a workspace looks exactly like one that does not exist.

## Seats

A team plan includes a number of **seats**. Every member and every pending invitation uses one, so
a full workspace cannot invite anyone else until an invitation is revoked, a member is removed, or
seats are added. Team includes five, and more are added in packs of five. See
[Plans and billing](/concepts/plans-and-billing).

## Limits worth knowing

- **Roles apply to the whole workspace.** There are no per-project roles yet: a Developer can work in every
  project in the workspace, and a Viewer can see all of them.
- **A person has one personal workspace**, but can own several team workspaces.
- **Members are people, not machines.** Machines are enrolled into a workspace and are shared by everyone
  allowed to use them.
