A team workspace is shared, so it has to answer three questions: **what happens the moment someone
loses access, who did what, and who can see what.**

## When someone is removed

Removing a member, or a member leaving, takes effect **immediately, not at their next sign-in**:

- Their live connections to the workspace, open log streams and shells, are closed.
- Their next request in that workspace is refused, and to them the workspace now looks like it does not
  exist (`workspace not found`).
- Any API key they created for the workspace stops working.
- Their other workspaces are **not affected**, and neither is their account.

What stays: everything they created belongs to the workspace and remains, and a deployment already in
progress is not interrupted. The activity log keeps every action they took, with their name and the role they held
at the time, for as long as the plan's activity retention lasts.

A removed person can be invited again later; their earlier history is reattached to them rather than
starting over.

<Note>
  A member can also be **suspended** rather than removed, when a plan change leaves fewer seats than people.
  Suspending is reversible and never deletes anyone. See [Downgrading and what happens](/guides/downgrading-and-what-happens).
</Note>

## What the activity log records

Every change made in a team workspace is recorded with:

- **who** did it (the person, not a shared account),
- their **role at that moment**,
- **what** they did and **which** thing they did it to,
- whether it was **allowed or denied**, and when.

This includes people-level events (invitations, role changes, removals, ownership offers), production actions and
approvals, and **every time a secret value is revealed**. The value itself is never written to the log.

Denied attempts are recorded as well as allowed ones, so a pattern of refused production deploys is visible.

The log keeps entries for a limited time set by your plan: **7 days on Free, 30 on Developer, 90 on Team**. Older
entries are cleared; that never affects your apps or deployments.

## Who can read it

| Role | Sees |
| --- | --- |
| Owner, Admin | Everyone's activity in the workspace |
| Developer, Viewer | Their own activity |

The workspace activity view merges audit events, deployments and operations into one feed. In the CLI, filter it
by person, action or time; see [`beaver workspace activity`](/cli/workspace#activity).

## What an admin can and cannot see

| Admins **can** see | Admins **cannot** see |
| --- | --- |
| Everything in the workspace, including its members' email addresses | Another member's **private** Alie conversations, unless the member shared them with the workspace |
| Everyone's activity, and pending invitations and approvals | Payment details and payment history, which are the Owner's alone |
| Secret values, subject to the [production safety](/concepts/production-safety-and-approvals) rules | Anything in a workspace they do not belong to |

Developers and Viewers see names but not email addresses in the member list.

## What Beaver support can see

To help you, VectorIHub staff can look at a workspace's plan, members, invitations and seat usage. They **cannot
sign in as you, read secret values, or read anyone's private Alie conversations**, and every support action on your
workspace is recorded and requires a written reason.

## Alerts reach the right people

Alerts about the workspace itself go to the people responsible for it, not to every member: a machine going offline
reaches Owners and Admins, and a billing problem reaches the Owner. Owners and Admins can change who receives each
kind, and each person can mute what they do not want.
