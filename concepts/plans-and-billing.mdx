Your Beaver plan sets usage limits. Billing covers what you're charged for **Beaver
itself** — separate from whatever your infrastructure provider (AWS, a VPS host, your own
hardware) charges you for the machines.

```bash
beaver plans
beaver billing
```

## The plans

Beaver has two plans today. **Free** was previously called Hobby — same plan, new name.

| | Free | Developer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | $0 | $15 / month, or $12 / month billed annually |
| Connected machines | 2 | Unlimited |
| Repositories | 5 | Unlimited |
| Deployable branches per repository | 1 | Unlimited |
| Deployments | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Managed services | 2 | 10 |
| Team members | 1 (you) | 1 (you) |
| Log and trace retention | 3 days | 14 days |
| Support | Community | Email |

Both plans include the full deploy workflow — connecting a machine, deploying, inspecting a
failure, and redeploying. The plans differ in how much you can run and how long history is
kept, not in whether the basics work.

<Note>
  Retention is how long Beaver keeps your logs and traces before they're cleared. It applies
  to history only — it never affects your running apps or your deployments.
</Note>

## Two bills, not one

Connecting your own machines means you pay your provider directly for them — Beaver's charge
is only for the platform. If your bill looks off, check which side of that split it's
actually on before assuming Beaver over- or under-charged you.

## What happens when a limit is reached

Reaching a limit blocks **adding** one more of that thing until you upgrade or free up room.
It never removes or stops something you already have — if your plan's limit drops below what
you're running, everything keeps running and you're only blocked from adding more.

`beaver billing subscription` shows your plan and how much of each limit you're using; a limit
you're past is marked *over limit*.
