Before a deployment actually runs, Beaver gives you three ways to check what it would do
first — from a fast local check to a full readiness sweep.

```bash
beaver validate
beaver plan <container>
beaver preflight
```

## Three checks, three depths

`beaver validate` checks your local `.beaver/config.yml` for errors — entirely offline,
entirely read-only. `beaver plan <container>` goes further: it resolves what a real deploy
of that container would do — the revision it would use, what would change — again without
doing it. `beaver preflight` runs the broadest sweep, across build, security, and other
check categories.

## Warning versus blocker

Every `preflight` finding is one of two things: a **warning** (the deploy can still proceed)
or a **blocker** (it can't, until you fix it or explicitly override where an override
exists). This distinction is the whole point of running preflight — it tells you which
findings you can ship past and which you can't.

## What none of this guarantees

None of these commands guarantee a deploy will succeed — they catch what's checkable ahead
of time. A deploy still validates again once it actually runs, since some conditions (like
another change landing in between) can only be known at that moment.
