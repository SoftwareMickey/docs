Beaver has three distinct feeds. They look similar, but they answer different questions, and
none of them overlap by accident.

## The three feeds

| Feed | Answers | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Following** | "What have people and templates I follow published?" | Strictly what you follow — never your own followers' activity, never anything unpublished. |
| **Explore** | "What's worth discovering that I don't follow yet?" | Every public template and post, ranked for relevance and recency. Includes a "Trending now" view for recent momentum specifically. |
| **Workspace activity** | "What happened in my workspace?" | Deployments, builds, and team actions — entirely separate from the two feeds above, and never mixed with them. |

Following and Explore both show only things someone explicitly **published** — nothing
routine (a deploy, a config change, an internal audit event) ever appears in either one on
its own. Workspace activity is the opposite: it's exactly that routine operational log, kept
in its own place on purpose.

## Following and unfollowing

You can follow a user, or a public template. Following a user surfaces everything *they*
publish; following a template surfaces new releases and forks *of that template*, regardless
of who forks it.

**Unfollowing — and blocking, and muting — leave no trace.** There is no "X unfollowed Y"
notice anywhere, to anyone. Blocking someone hides your activity from them and theirs from
you in both directions; muting only ever affects your own feed and is invisible to the muted
account. Know this before relying on it: if you're expecting a visible signal when you
unfollow or block someone, there isn't one, deliberately.

## Publishing

Publishing turns a private project into a public (or unlisted) **template** — something
anyone can deploy into their own workspace with one action, or fork into a draft they
customize before publishing themselves. See [Publishing a
template](/guides/publishing-a-template) for exactly what becomes public and what to
double-check before you do it — the short version is: every publish shows you a preview of
the exact fields that become public before anything commits, and environment variable
**values** are never included, only their names.

A **fork** keeps attribution back to the original template, however many times it's
re-published down the chain — every deployer can always see where a template originally came
from.

## Share links and your analytics

Every public post or template can be shared with a link. When someone opens your link, signs
up through it, or deploys what you shared, Beaver credits that link to you — so you can see
which things you publish actually get people using Beaver, not just looking at it.

Publishers get an **analytics** tab on their own profile: views, deploys, forks, saves,
comments, and sign-ups that came in through your links, for the last 7 days, 30 days, or all
time. Only you can see it. Views appear there and in your digest — **never as an individual
notification**, and opening your own link doesn't count.

## Verified creators

Some publishers carry a small **verified** badge on their profile, their templates, and their
cards in your feeds. It means the Beaver team has confirmed them as a known, trusted
publisher, and their templates rank slightly higher in Explore. It's granted by Beaver
operators only — there's no application form or self-service way to get it.

## Reporting and moderation

Reporting a piece of content (a post, a template, or an account) sends it to platform
operators for review — reporting is never visible to the person or content you reported, and
doesn't remove anything by itself. An operator reviewing your report may dismiss it or act on
it; either way, nothing about *your* report is ever shown to the reported party.
