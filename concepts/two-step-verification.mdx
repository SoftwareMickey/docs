2-step verification adds a second proof to signing in with a password, Google or GitHub: a six-digit
code from an authenticator app, or a **passkey or security key**. Anyone who learns your password
still cannot sign in without it.

## What it protects, and what it does not

<Warning>
The codes are one-time passwords. They stop password reuse and leaked-password attacks. They are
**not phishing-resistant**: someone who tricks you into typing a code into a fake page can use it
within its short lifetime. Check the address bar before you type a code. A passkey does not have this
weakness — see below.
</Warning>

- **Protected:** every sign-in to the web app, and approving a new CLI or Desktop sign-in.
- **Re-checked at the moment:** a few sensitive actions ask for a fresh code even when you are
  signed in (see the table below).
- **Not covered:** a session that is already signed in keeps working until it ends or is signed out.
  This is why changing or turning off the factor signs out every other session.

## Passkeys and security keys

A passkey (your fingerprint, face or screen lock) or a hardware security key is bound to Beaver's
real address by your browser. A look-alike site cannot ask it for anything, so unlike a code it
**cannot be phished**.

- **Add one** under Settings → Security → Passkeys and security keys. You can hold up to ten, alongside
  an authenticator app. Adding one when you already have a factor asks you to confirm with it first, so a
  stolen session cannot enrol someone else's key.
- **At sign-in** the browser shows its own prompt. An authenticator code and your recovery codes stay
  available as fallbacks; an account with only passkeys is never shown a code box.
- **Synced passkeys** (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager, a password manager) work on your other
  devices and survive losing one. A hardware key is a single object: register two.
- **Removing** one signs your other browsers and devices out. Removing the last one turns 2-step
  verification off, with the same confirmation and the same sign-out as turning it off directly.
- **Lost every passkey?** Recovery codes and the email recovery below work exactly as they do for an
  authenticator app, and they remove passkeys too.
- **CLI and Desktop** never prompt for a passkey — the browser does. A device approved with one is
  labelled "Approved with a passkey".

<Note>
If a key reports a signature counter that goes backwards, it may have been copied. A hardware key is
refused and the event appears in your Security activity; remove it and add a new one. Synced passkeys
do not report a meaningful counter, so they are never refused for it.
</Note>

## Recovery codes

Turning it on shows ten recovery codes once. Each works once, in place of an authenticator code.
Keep them somewhere that is not your phone. Generating a new set retires the old one. Signing in
with a recovery code signs your other browsers out and marks your devices for review.

## If you lose both

You have two routes. Both end with 2-step verification removed and every session signed out, so you
sign in again and set it up fresh.

1. **Email recovery.** Ask for it on the sign-in page. A link is emailed to the account address; confirm
   it within one hour. Removal then happens after a **72-hour wait**, during which every confirmation
   email says how to cancel. The page gives the same answer whether or not an account exists, so it
   cannot be used to look people up.
2. **Support.** Support can reset it for you after you answer verification questions. A reset needs
   a written reason, a second support person to approve it, and a 24-hour delay (skipped if you
   prove you control the mailbox). You are told and can cancel during the delay.

## Workspaces that require it

An Owner can require 2-step verification for a workspace. Members who already have it are
unaffected. Members who do not get a **grace period** (7 days by default, 0–30, set by the Owner)
counted from the later of when the rule turned on and when they joined, with reminders at 3 days and
1 day. After the deadline the workspace is blocked for them until they turn it on. A member who just
had their factor removed by recovery or a support reset gets **no grace**, since they were secure
before. If you are in several workspaces, the earliest deadline applies.

While a workspace requires it, approving a CLI or Desktop sign-in for that account needs the factor
on first.

### Requiring a passkey

An Owner can choose the stricter **Passkey or security key only** level instead of "Authenticator app or
passkey". Only a session that **signed in with a passkey** satisfies it; a session that used a code or a
recovery code is refused with `MFA_PASSKEY_REQUIRED` until the person signs in again with their passkey.
Members who only have an authenticator app get a fresh grace period when the Owner tightens the rule,
and CLI or Desktop sign-ins must be approved with a passkey too. The Owner needs a passkey themselves
and must be signed in with it to turn this on, so the change can never lock them out.

## Audit trail

Your account's **Security activity** list shows sign-ins, code failures, changes and recovery events
for your account. Workspace events are purged on the workspace's audit-retention schedule; account
security events currently have no automatic purge.
