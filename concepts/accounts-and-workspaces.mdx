Your **account** is your identity — one login, used everywhere. Your **workspace** is the
top-level scope everything else (projects, machines, services) lives inside. For a solo
user these feel like the same thing, because logging in creates a personal workspace
automatically. If you work with others you can also belong to team workspaces; see
[Workspaces and teams](/concepts/workspaces-and-teams).

```bash
beaver login
beaver whoami
```

## Why this matters

You rarely think about workspaces day to day — until you're onboarding a teammate, deciding
what they can see, or you hit a wall where something you created isn't visible from a
different login. Knowing the difference between "my account" and "my workspace" is what
makes that make sense instead of feeling arbitrary.

## What you can see and control

`beaver login` authenticates your account; `beaver whoami` shows who you're currently signed
in as, and which workspace you're in. Everything you create — projects, connected machines,
services — belongs to a workspace. In a team workspace, the people you invite see the same
resources, scoped by their [role](/concepts/roles-and-permissions).

## What deleting a workspace actually means

<Warning>
  Deleting a workspace is not the same as logging out. It removes every project, deployment,
  and connected-machine registration inside it. The machines themselves keep running —
  Beaver doesn't reach out and stop anything on your infrastructure — but Beaver stops
  managing them until they're reconnected to a workspace again. Recreating a workspace
  starts empty; it doesn't restore what a deleted one had.
</Warning>
