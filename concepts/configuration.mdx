Your project's deployment settings — build strategy, health checks, replicas, resources,
networks, volumes — live in one file, **`.beaver/config.yml`**, in your project directory.
Beaver also keeps a **saved copy** of this configuration per container, separate from your
local file, which is what actually gets applied when you or a teammate deploys.

```bash
beaver config show
beaver config status
```

## Local versus saved — the distinction that matters

Your local file is what you edit and commit. The saved copy is what Beaver actually deploys
from. These two can disagree — someone changed something from the dashboard, or you edited
locally but haven't pushed yet — and Beaver shows you exactly which fields differ rather than
just telling you something's "out of sync." Keeping them in agreement is explicit: push your
local edits, or pull the saved copy down to match it.

## What you can see and control

`.beaver/config.yml` can be generated (inferred from your Dockerfile/Compose file), authored
by hand, or edited field by field via the CLI. A JSON Schema for the file's shape is
available for editor autocomplete and validation — it describes your local YAML file's
structure, not any server-side behavior.

## What isn't tracked

Some fields — like which Dockerfile path to build from — aren't part of the saved
configuration at all. Pulling never overwrites them, since there's nothing saved to pull;
they only ever come from your local file.
