**Alie** is Beaver's infrastructure AI. You talk to it about your infrastructure — what is running, why something looks wrong — and it can **propose actions** with real effects, such as a redeploy or a scaling change.
You can reach it from the CLI, from Beaver Desktop (an Alie panel beside whatever you are looking at) and from the Dashboard.

## The one rule that matters

Alie's actions go through **the same approval and policy controls a human-typed command would**. Depending on policy, an action waits for your explicit confirmation before it runs; everything waiting for you is listed in one place.
An approval confirms that Alie may proceed with **one specific proposed action** — it does not grant broader access. Review what is proposed before approving, the same way you would before running the equivalent command yourself.

## What it works with

- **Your own AI provider key.** Alie uses a provider connection you set up — see [Connect an AI provider](/alie/connect-an-ai-provider).
- **Sessions.** A chat is a session you can resume, fork, rename, archive or delete — see [Sessions and context](/alie/sessions-and-context).
- **Workspace context.** Knowledge rules, runbooks, goals and policies your workspace defines are applied across conversations.

## How much it can do on its own

You decide how far Alie can act without asking, at three levels: per tool, workspace-wide (autopilot) and per area in Desktop. The default is cautious.
See [Approvals and safety](/alie/approvals-and-safety), [Automation policies](/alie/automation-policies) and [Safe production use](/alie/safe-production-use).

## What this documentation does not describe

How Alie decides to use a tool, which tools are wired up internally and how approvals are resolved on the server are internal to the platform. These pages cover what you can see, ask for and configure.
