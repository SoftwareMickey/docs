# Deployment Configuration

Beyond your Dockerfile, a handful of settings tell VectorIHub how to run and monitor your container. These are set once in the dashboard and apply regardless of which infrastructure provider you deploy to.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Port" icon="plug">
    The port your app listens on inside the container.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Health Checks" icon="heart-pulse">
    A path VectorIHub polls to confirm your app is healthy before routing traffic to it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Resources" icon="cpu">
    CPU and memory limits for your container.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

---

## Setting Your Configuration

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Project Dashboard">
    Go to your project inside the **VectorIHub Dashboard**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Navigate to Deployment Settings">
    Open **Settings → Deployment Configuration**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Your Port">
    Match this to the port your Dockerfile `EXPOSE`s and your app listens on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a Health Check Path (Recommended)">
    A simple `200 OK` endpoint (e.g. `/health`) is enough — VectorIHub uses it to gate zero-downtime deploys.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>Setting a health check path enables blue-green deployments — your previous version stays live until the new one passes its health check.</Tip>

## Why This Isn't in Your Dockerfile

Your Dockerfile describes how your app is *built*. Port, health checks, and resource limits describe how VectorIHub should *run and monitor* it once deployed — separating the two means you can adjust deployment behavior without changing your build.
