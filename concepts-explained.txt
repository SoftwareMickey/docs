• Yes—explain Beaver Cloud and Services as concepts, alongside the commands. Commands teach users how to act; concept pages help them decide what to use and understand
  the consequences.

  For each, cover:

  - What it is and which user problem it solves.
  - When to use it, with a practical example.
  - What users need beforehand, such as connected machines.
  - How it relates to projects, containers, and other features.
  - User-visible behavior and limitations, including persistence, connectivity, and redeploy requirements where applicable.
  - Links to a getting-started workflow and the command reference.

  Keep the explanation at the product level. For Services, users need to understand what connecting PostgreSQL to their application accomplishes and when the application
  can use it. They don’t need the internal attachment schema, credential provisioning mechanism, or network implementation.

  A useful structure is concept → task guide → command reference. That gives beginners context and experienced users precise options without exposing the engineering
  behind the product.
