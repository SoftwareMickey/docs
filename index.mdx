**Beaver** deploys your Dockerized application to infrastructure you own — a VPS, AWS, Google Cloud,
Azure or any Linux machine. You describe how the app is built with a **Dockerfile**; Beaver builds it, runs it
on your machines, and gives you one place to deploy, observe, secure and recover it. Beaver is the product;
**VectorIHub** is the platform behind it ([what that means](/start/what-is-vectorihub)).

<Tip>
**New here?** [Choose your infrastructure](/start/choose-your-infrastructure), then follow the
[quickstart](/start/quickstart-dashboard). Most people have an app running in about fifteen minutes.
</Tip>

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a Dockerfile">
    Your Dockerfile is the single source of truth for how the app is built and run. Beaver does not generate one or
    detect your framework — see [Dockerfile requirements](/deploy/dockerfile-requirements).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect a machine">
    Run `beaver connect` on a machine you own and choose its role. [Connect a machine](/infrastructure/connect-a-machine).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Group machines into a Beaver Cloud">
    A deployment runs in a [Beaver Cloud](/infrastructure/beaver-cloud) — a group of your machines.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deploy and watch it">
    Deploy from a local project or a connected GitHub repository, then follow it through status, logs and
    metrics. [Deploy overview](/deploy/overview).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Three ways to work, one workspace

Beaver has a web **Dashboard**, a **Desktop** app and a **CLI**. They act on the same workspace, so you can start in
one and continue in another. The [CLI reference](/cli) documents every command.

## Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need a Dockerfile?">
    Yes. Your Dockerfile defines the build, runtime and startup command, which keeps builds predictable and works
    for any language that can produce a Docker image.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Beaver host my app?">
    No. Beaver deploys to machines you own and pay your provider for; Beaver's plan covers Beaver itself.
    See [Plans and billing](/teams/plans-and-billing).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do updates reach production?">
    You choose how the switch happens: a rolling strategy keeps the previous version running until the new one
    passes its health check; a recreate strategy stops it first. See the
    [deployment lifecycle](/deploy/deployment-lifecycle).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I move to another provider later?">
    The application definition does not change when the machines do. See [Providers](/infrastructure/providers).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
